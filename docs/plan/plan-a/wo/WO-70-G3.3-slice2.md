### Objective and business value

Verified at HEAD=`3a44087`: nothing in this codebase notices when a
parser CHANGE silently changes the figures extracted from a statement
that already registered. `fuel_ingest.ingest_transaction` (WO-50) is
deliberately insert-or-no-op on the natural key `(org, entity,
supplier, period, line_seq)` — so re-ingesting a statement after a
parser edit whose output drifted writes NOTHING and warns NOBODY: the
stored rows keep their old figures, the new parse's divergent figures
vanish, and no surface records that the extractor no longer reproduces
the numbers a claim was built from. `BA_fleet_fuel.md` §2.1a names the
missing control verbatim: *"**Anti-drift:** `extraction_baseline`
records a confirmed extraction as known-good; `regression_check` flags
a drift when a re-extraction moves net or vat by more than 0.02"*
(Appendix B: *"Regression drift: > 0.02 on net or vat"*), and
`ARCH_plan.md`'s G3.3 third paragraph carries the same sentence.
WO-66 explicitly deferred it ("NOT part of R25's acceptance; it needs
its own baseline store and a re-extraction caller that does not exist
yet").

Who stops losing money: `ARCH_plan.md` T-6 rates parser accuracy the
moat-level risk — seven networks with learned quirks are now live
(G3.2 closed), and every future parser edit (a quirk fix for one
network's format revision) can silently perturb another code path.
The tie-out (WO-66) only catches drift where a human typed an
expectation for that (supplier, period); the extraction baseline is
the AUTOMATIC memory of every statement that ever registered — the
regression net that makes "PASS = trained" stay true after training.
A 3-cent drift on a statement's parsed VAT is a mis-keyed legal
filing waiting for a re-close; flagging it at the first re-extraction
is how the product keeps the "every promise dies if a number is
wrong" contract without a human re-typing anything.

### Design decisions (from the harvested spec — cite, then decide)

1. **What is baselined.** Per-(statement × currency) extraction
   aggregates: `line_count`, `net_total`, `vat_total` — computed from
   the PARSED, pre-FX, LOCAL-currency figures (`q2` of the `Decimal`
   sum over `ParsedFuelLine.net_local` / `.vat_local`). The spec's
   drift metrics are exactly "net or vat" (§2.1a, Appendix B) — so net
   and vat are harvested; two column choices are documented
   interpretations: (a) LOCAL currency, not EUR — FX resolution is
   downstream of extraction and depends on the mutable ECB rate cache,
   so an EUR-basis baseline would flag rate-cache changes as parser
   drift (false alarms — against the "fail toward not crying wolf"
   posture); the parser's own output IS the extraction. (b) PER
   CURRENCY — master-context §4.14 forbids summing local amounts
   across currencies and WO-64 (Q8) proved one statement can carry
   several; the same interpretation WO-66 documented for the tie-out
   grain. `line_count` (exact compare) is a third documented
   interpretation: a parser change that splits or merges lines can
   drift compensatingly with equal totals, and a dropped/duplicated
   line is T-6's worst failure — recording the count costs one
   integer.
2. **Keyed how.** `(org_id, statement_sha256, currency)` — the
   SHA-256 of the statement bytes IS the identity, because
   "re-extraction" means *the same document, possibly different parser
   code*. Keying by (entity, supplier, period) would compare two
   DIFFERENT uploads and manufacture drift out of legitimately
   different files. `network` and `filename` are stored for display
   only. Deliberately NO `entity_id`: parsing never sees the entity
   (`fuel_card_parser.run(filename, content)`), so the baseline is a
   property of (org, bytes) — and stays comparable if the same file is
   ever re-registered under a corrected entity. Tenant-scoped
   (org-keyed) because the figures derive from a tenant's document —
   cross-org visibility of another tenant's statement totals would be
   a leak (§4.1).
3. **When the baseline is written.** At the FIRST successful
   `ingest_statement` registration of that digest — after the capture
   review gate passed, every line's FX resolved, and the rows written.
   That is this codebase's port of "a CONFIRMED extraction": the
   registration act is the human-initiated confirm (same reading as
   WO-66's "the registration block"). Never overwritten implicitly —
   a later matching ingest COMPARES instead of re-recording.
   `rebaseline` (re-parse the supplied bytes, replace the stored
   figures, audited old→new) is the ONLY overwrite; `remove_baseline`
   (audited) is the fail-open mitigation. Both refuse an unknown
   digest with an opaque `NotFoundError` (§4.4).
4. **What triggers a re-extraction comparison.** Two callers: (a)
   `ingest_statement` itself, when the digest already has baseline
   rows — the natural retry/re-upload path, where the comparison
   matters MOST because `ingest_transaction`'s insert-or-no-op
   semantics would otherwise make a changed parse invisible (see
   Objective); (b) the explicit service
   `regression_check(db, org_id, *, filename, content)` — the
   "re-extraction caller" WO-66 named as missing: parse the supplied
   bytes through the deterministic registry, compare against the
   stored baseline, return per-currency findings. Read-only — it
   records nothing, mutates nothing (a pure check an eventual route
   or CI-style sweep can call repeatedly).
5. **What happens on drift (>0.02).** ADVISORY — a warning, never a
   block. The harvested verb is *"flags a drift"*, in deliberate
   contrast to the same paragraph's two R25 regimes which "block"
   registration and "halt" the close; WO-66 records that anti-drift
   is NOT part of R25's acceptance. §4.19's advisory semantics
   therefore apply: deterministic, nothing self-adjusts, no figure is
   mutated or gated — the flag surfaces in
   `StatementIngestResult.warnings` (this slice's established review
   surface) and in `RegressionCheckResult.findings`, and a HUMAN
   decides: fix the parser, or `rebaseline` (audited) if the new
   figures are the corrected truth. Threshold is STRICTLY greater:
   "moves … by MORE than 0.02" ⇒ `abs(q2(new) − q2(old)) >
   Decimal("0.02")` flags; a move of exactly 0.02 does not (the
   mirror of the batch tie's `<= 0.02` allowed), compared on
   quantized Decimals on both sides (the WO-66 boundary-noise
   discipline). A `line_count` mismatch flags at ANY difference
   (exact, per decision 1). A currency present in the baseline but
   absent from the re-extraction — or vice versa — flags (the row
   set itself is part of what was known-good).

### Implementation guidance

1. **Model.** Columns: `org_id` (GUID, FK organizations CASCADE,
   indexed), `statement_sha256` String(64) NOT NULL,
   `currency` String(3) NOT NULL, `network` String(60) NOT NULL,
   `filename` String(255) NOT NULL, `line_count` Integer NOT NULL
   CHECK `>= 0`, `net_total` Numeric(14, 2) NOT NULL, `vat_total`
   Numeric(14, 2) NOT NULL. Unique
   `(org_id, statement_sha256, currency)`
   (`uq_fuel_extraction_baselines_key`). No composite entity FK —
   decision 2. Money columns are 2-dp because the stored figure is a
   `q2`-quantized comparison target, never an input to further
   arithmetic (the drift compare re-quantizes both sides anyway).
2. **Aggregation is pure and per-currency.** `_aggregate(parsed) ->
   dict[currency, (line_count, net_total, vat_total)]` — `Decimal`
   sums over the unrounded parsed figures, `q2` applied to the SUM
   (the `capture_review` batch-tie discipline: quantize the total,
   not the addends). Never a float, never a cross-currency sum
   (§4.9/§4.14).
3. **`compare(baselines, parsed)` is pure** (no DB, no clock):
   returns `DriftFinding(currency, metric, baseline, actual,
   message)` tuples — metrics `lines` (exact), `net` / `vat`
   (strictly `> DRIFT_TOLERANCE` on q2'd Decimals), and `currency`
   (set mismatch in either direction). Empty tuple = no drift.
4. **`record` is the confirm-time writer.** Called ONLY from
   `ingest_statement` when no baseline rows exist for (org, digest).
   One row per currency; ONE audit event per statement
   (`TRANSPORT_EXTRACTION_BASELINE_SET`, meta: sha256, network,
   filename, `old=None`, `new={currency: {lines, net, vat}}`) — the
   statement is the operation's natural grain, unlike the tie-out's
   per-row human typing.
5. **`regression_check` fails toward the module gate first**, like
   every transport service (`module_not_enabled` PermissionError),
   validates by parsing (`ValueError` →
   `ValidationError(code="unrecognized_fuel_card_statement")`, the
   `ingest_statement` convention), then: no baseline rows ⇒
   `baselined=False, findings=()` — NOT an error (an unbaselined
   statement is simply not checked; fail-OPEN by absence, the WO-66
   posture) — else `baselined=True` + `compare()` findings.
6. **`rebaseline` re-parses, never re-types.** The tie-out's figures
   are human-typed BECAUSE independence from the parser is its point;
   the baseline's figures are BY DEFINITION the parser's own output,
   so `rebaseline(db, org_id, *, filename, content)` re-parses and
   replaces the (org, digest) row set (update matched currencies,
   insert new, delete vanished), audited old→new in one event.
   Refuses an unknown digest (`baseline_not_found`) — a baseline
   exists only for a statement that actually registered (decision 3).
   `remove_baseline(db, org_id, *, statement_sha256)` deletes the
   row set, audited; opaque 404 when absent or foreign (§4.4).
7. **Ingest wiring placement: AFTER phase 2 + entity learning.** The
   baseline records a CONFIRMED (fully registered) extraction — a
   statement refused by the capture gate or FX resolution writes no
   rows and must leave no baseline. On the re-seen path, drift
   warnings prefix `"extraction drift: "` and append AFTER the
   parser/review warnings (stable ordering for the tests). The
   drift path never raises and never blocks (decision 5); the
   record path can only fail with the session (same transaction —
   §4.16's "the caller's commit persists it atomically").
8. **Digest.** `hashlib.sha256(content).hexdigest()` — computed once
   in `ingest_statement`, passed through; `regression_check`
   computes its own from the caller's bytes.
9. **Migration** copies `b3f1c6d2a904`'s RLS block verbatim
   (ENABLE + FORCE + `tenant_isolation` policy under the
   `app.current_org` predicate, Postgres-only guard). Downgrade
   drops policy + index + table.
10. **Tenancy-parity classification in the SAME commit as the
    table** (the WO-66 CI failure lesson): `EXEMPT["fuel_extraction_
    baselines"]` with the no-route-reachable-read-path reasoning +
    the "gains a probe with the first transport route" hand-off,
    matching the ten existing transport entries.

  `regression_check` in org B reports `baselined=False` for org A's

- **§4.14:** per-currency rows; no cross-currency aggregation ever.
- **§4.16:** every baseline mutation (record/rebaseline/remove) emits
  one audit event with old→new in the same transaction. The drift
  CHECK writes nothing and is deliberately not audited (a read).
- **§4.19 (advisory semantics):** drift flags NEVER block
  registration, never mutate a figure, never gate a close; nothing
  self-adjusts — `rebaseline` is an explicit, audited human act.
- **ADR-P3 (domain isolation):** the new service imports only
  transport models + the platform floor (`audit`, `modules`,
  `money`, `errors`); `test_boundaries.py` stays green.
- **WO-50's insert-or-no-op ingestion is unchanged** — the baseline
  observes it (that invisibility is the motivation), never alters it.

### Rollback strategy

and advisory — no caller shape changed). Migration downgrade is
written and tested (drops the table; baselines are lost but

recovery). Narrow mitigation without revert: `remove_baseline` for a
noisy digest (that statement is then simply unchecked — fail-open by
absence, the same posture as WO-66's expectation removal).
