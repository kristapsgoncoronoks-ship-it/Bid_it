### Objective and business value

`README.md` (lines 12-14, 17, 102-106) and `ARCHITECTURE.md` (lines 4-6, item 2)
assert specific counts — Alembic revisions, model/service/route modules, SPA
pages, collected tests, CI job count and names, ADR count — that no longer
match the live tree (table above). These are the two docs a new contributor,
a prospective engineering hire doing due diligence, or a future AI work-order
agent is most likely to skim first (`00_MASTER_CONTEXT.md` §2 even calls out
that the *previous* version of these files was "materially stale" and points
readers to `docs/` instead — but the current, truth-up'd version still invites
being cited for these numbers, since nothing flags it as wrong to a casual
reader). Wrong module/test counts are low-severity but they are exactly the
kind of small, checkable-in-30-seconds inaccuracy that erodes trust in a repo
whose entire pitch (per `00_MASTER_CONTEXT.md` §7.7) is "no claim of
production-readiness without evidence."

Nobody's invoice or refund is wrong because of this. The business value is
narrow and specific: (a) a due-diligence reader (an investor's technical
advisor, a new senior hire) who spot-checks one of these numbers and finds it
wrong reasonably discounts every other unverified claim in the repo, which is
a disproportionately expensive trust cost for a one-engineer shop; (b) adding
a live-tree-checked regression test converts this from a recurring manual
chore (this is the second time these exact numbers have drifted) into a
one-time fix.
