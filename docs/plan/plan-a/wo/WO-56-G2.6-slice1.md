# WO-56 — G2.6 slice 1: hard period-end gate + Art. 17 minimum-amount gate + deadline risk scanner (board G2.6, R7/R8/R9)

**WORK ORDER 56 — G2.6 slice 1: the hard period-end gate (R7), the Art. 17
minimum-amount gate (R8), and the 30-Sep deadline risk scanner (R9). Effort M
(3-5d). Priority P0. Milestone M3. Depends on: WO-54 (G2.5 — frozen claim
lines, whose `preview_vat_base` twin this order adds).**

### Objective and business value

WO-49-55 shipped the claim grain, the lock, the claim-line builder, the
freeze, and the goods-code mapping, but `lock.submit_claim` still accepted
ANY draft claim regardless of whether its filing period had ended or whether
its VAT amount cleared the statutory minimum — verified at HEAD=`c5bf573`
(this order's baseline, 1322 passed/10 skipped): no `app/services/transport/
deadline.py` or `minimum.py` exists anywhere in the tree, and `submit_claim`
has exactly two gates (`claim_not_draft`, `empty_claim_set`). `ARCH_plan.md`'s
own risk register scores the missed-30-Sep-deadline scenario **L-1 at 6 — the
maximum score in the entire document** ("a fatal time-bar; the client's loss
is directly attributable to us... North-star KPI: deadline misses = 0").
R7/R8/R9 (`BA_fleet_fuel.md` A3-A5) are the three simplest, most self-
contained slices of the larger G2.6 gate stack — pure date/currency
arithmetic, no new tables, no dependency on the still-decision-gated
"customer/fee" concept (`docs/DECISIONS-NEEDED.md` §10) that blocks G2.9.

Who pays more without this: every draft claim today could be submitted the
day it was created, for one cent of VAT, regardless of whether its period
had even finished — neither of which a refunding tax authority would accept,
and both of which are silent, structural gaps in exactly the vertical this
milestone is being built to defend. Landing R7/R8 now, independently of the
larger (and partly decision-gated) G2.6 stack, closes the single highest-
scored risk in the whole M3 plan without waiting on G2.9's unresolved
commercial question.

### Implementation guidance

1. **Why `preview_vat_base` had to be built, not reused as-is.** Fleet
   Fuel's own submission gate order (D5) checks the minimum-amount gate
   BEFORE freezing/locking a claim — a below-minimum claim must never
   freeze at all. `freeze_claim_lines` both COMPUTES and MUTATES (stamps
   `frozen_at`, writes `claim.vat_eur`/etc) in one pass; the minimum gate
   needs the computed figure WITHOUT the mutation. Extracting the shared
   arithmetic into `_sum_lines` (used by both) means the previewed figure
   and the frozen figure can never drift apart — the same drift-prevention
   discipline `is_synthetic()`'s centralization exists for.
2. **`NATIONAL_MINIMUMS` lists only the two entries `BA_fleet_fuel.md` A3
   actually gives (Sweden, Denmark).** The source text independently
   confirms euro countries AND Poland are deliberately absent (EUR-base
   fallback verified) but says nothing about completeness for any OTHER
   non-euro member state (Hungary, Czechia, Romania, Bulgaria, ...).
   Extending the table is flagged as future work in both the module
   docstring and a dedicated test
   (`test_g2_6_min_for_poland_is_deliberately_absent_falls_back_to_eur`),
   not silently assumed to already be handled.
3. **Every pre-existing test that calls `submit_claim` needed re-auditing
   against the new gates — this is the expected, DESIGNED consequence of
   landing a new legal gate, not a weakened test.** Per master-context §9
   ("if a test breaks... if it was asserting correct behaviour, your change
   is wrong... if fixture privilege needs raising, raise it — never weaken
   the assertion"): every failing fixture was using unrealistically small
   VAT amounts (tens of euros) that a real fuel-VAT claim would never
   actually have; the FIX is `override_minimum=True` (an explicit, visible,
   commented escape hatch — never a deleted assertion) on the call sites
   whose test PURPOSE is the lock/freeze/withdraw mechanics, not R8 itself.
   One test (`test_g2_5_lost_lock_race_leaves_the_claim_unfrozen`) needed
   special care: its original `pytest.raises(Exception)` was broad enough
   to ALSO catch the new `ConflictError` from the minimum gate, which would
   have made the test pass for the WRONG reason (never reaching the actual
   lock race) — `override_minimum=True` was required there specifically to
   keep the test proving what it claims to prove.
4. **One fixture bug this order caught and fixed while testing on real
   Postgres:** `test_transport_lock_concurrency.py`'s third claim
   (`claim_c`, the withdraw-then-reclaim scenario) used `ref_period=
   "2026-Q3"` — a period that had NOT yet ended relative to the session's
   real wall-clock date, so the new period-end gate correctly refused it.
   Moved to `"2025-Q4"` (safely in the past); this is a fixture correction,
   not a weakening — the test's actual assertion (a third claim can lock an
   invoice a withdrawn claim released) is unchanged.
5. **Verified on real Postgres, not just SQLite** (master-context §5:
   "run whenever you touch tenancy, migrations, locks or numbering" — this
   order touches the locking function `submit_claim` directly). A scratch
   Postgres 16 cluster (non-root `pguser`, port 5433, NOSUPERUSER `appuser`)
   confirmed `tests/test_rls.py`, `tests/test_numbering_concurrency.py`,
   `tests/test_transport_lock_concurrency.py`,
   `tests/test_payment_run_pay_concurrency.py`,
   `tests/test_credit_note_lock_concurrency.py`,
   `tests/test_expense_decision_concurrency.py`, and
   `tests/test_rls_connection_reuse.py` all pass together against a FRESH
   database (an earlier combined run against a database left over from a
   prior attempt hit an unrelated `ix_users_email` collision from re-running
   the same suite twice without resetting state — confirmed as a pre-
   existing test-isolation characteristic, not a regression, by re-running
   against a fresh DB). Cluster torn down after use per the standing
   convention.

### Invariants this order must preserve

- **§4.9/§4.10 (money quantized, server recomputes)** — `preview_vat_base`
  reuses the exact same `q2`-quantizing `_sum_lines` helper `freeze_claim_
  lines` already used; no new rounding path.
- **§4.14 (no aggregate sums across currencies without a recorded
  conversion)** — `below_minimum` compares the claim's `vat_local` ONLY for
  `NATIONAL_MINIMUMS` countries (never a live FX guess), `vat_eur` for every
  other country — proven by `test_g2_6_a_national_minimum_country_compares_
  local_currency_not_eur` (a claim whose EUR value clears €400 but whose
  SEK value is below 4000 SEK is still blocked).
- **§4.16 (every mutating operation is audited)** — unchanged; the two new
  gates only ADD a refusal path before any mutation, they do not touch the
  existing `TRANSPORT_CLAIM_SUBMIT` audit call.
- **R7/R8/R9 verbatim case-law boundaries** — `SEK 3,999` blocked / `SEK
  4,000` allowed; `€399.99` blocked / `€400.00` allowed; period-end is a
  strict `>`, not `>=`; the deadline risk window is inclusive at exactly 60
  days.
- **ADR-P3 rule 2 (reads the core through services, never joins)** — both
  new modules make zero DB queries at all (pure functions);
  `test_transport_services_do_not_import_other_domain_models` stays green.

### Database / migration impact

None. No new table, column, or index. `submit_claim`'s new `override_
minimum`/`today` parameters are additive keyword-only arguments with safe
defaults (`False`/`None`) — every existing caller that omits them keeps its
previous behavior MINUS the two new gates now applying (which is the whole
point of this order).

### Testing requirements

`backend/tests/transport/test_g2_6_deadline.py` (8 tests): `period_end_date`
for every quarter + the year shape; `period_ended`'s strict `>` boundary (the
period-end day itself does NOT count as ended); `filing_deadline` is always

`backend/tests/transport/test_g2_6_minimum.py` (8 tests): `min_for` returns
the local basis for Sweden, the EUR basis for a euro country, and the EUR
basis for Poland specifically (verifying the "deliberately absent" claim);
the exact case-law boundaries (`SEK 3,999`/`4,000`, `€399.99`/`€400.00`);
the annual threshold is lower than the quarterly one; a `None` local amount
is treated as zero when the basis is local.

`backend/tests/transport/test_g2_6_submission_gates.py` (5 tests):
`submit_claim` refuses before period-end and succeeds once it has passed;
refuses a below-minimum claim (nothing frozen/locked); the override submits
and records the comparison in `status_note`; a national-minimum country
compares local currency, not EUR, even when the EUR conversion would clear
the base threshold.

### Rollback strategy

Code-only revert: remove the two gate blocks from `submit_claim` and delete
`deadline.py`/`minimum.py`/`preview_vat_base`. No migration to downgrade (none
created). The fixture changes (`override_minimum=True` additions,
`test_transport_lock_concurrency.py`'s period fix) would need reverting
alongside the code to keep the test suite internally consistent, but neither
direction breaks anything structurally. No kill switch needed for a
production incident: the `transport` module stays default-OFF and is absent
from every plan's module set, so `submit_claim` (gates and all) is
unreachable by any tenant regardless of code presence.
